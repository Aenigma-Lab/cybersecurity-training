Setting a SIM PIN is excellent for device-level security, but it does not fully prevent "SIM swap" fraud. There is a critical difference between locking your physical SIM card and locking your account with the carrier. 
1. What a SIM PIN Protects
A SIM PIN (set on your phone's settings) protects the physical chip.

2. Why it Doesn't Stop a "SIM Swap"
In a SIM swap attack, a hacker doesn't need your physical phone. They trick the carrier into "moving" your phone number to a new SIM card they already have. Since they are using a new chip, your old SIM PIN does nothing to stop them.

3. How to Actually Stop Unauthorized Swaps
To prevent a hacker from hijacking your number through the carrier, you must enable Account-Level protections: 
Carrier Account PIN: Set a unique PIN/Passcode with your carrier (e.g., AT&T, T-Mobile). This is required whenever someone calls customer service or visits a store to make changes like a SIM swap.
SIM Protection/Number Lock: Specific features like Verizon's Number Lock or T-Mobile's Account Takeover Protection explicitly block any attempt to transfer your number to a new SIM or carrier until you manually unlock it.
Port-Out PIN: Most carriers now require a special "Transfer PIN" (often valid for only a few days) before a number can be moved to a different provider.
