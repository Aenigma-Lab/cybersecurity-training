Fake UPI Overpayment Scam Awareness

Learn to identify fake UPI messages claiming extra money sent by mistake and protect your money.

How Fake UPI SMS Fraud Happens

Scammers send deceptive SMS messages that look like official UPI notifications.

Fake Payment Requests

You receive an SMS claiming a payment of ₹X has been requested from your UPI ID. Panic makes you click the malicious link.

Fake Sms claim

SMS claims your UPI account will be blocked unless you update KYC via their fake link.

Fake Prize Winnings

"Congratulations! You've won ₹1,000,000" - Just a trick to steal your UPI credentials.

Examples of Fake Overpayment SMS Messages

These are examples of fake SMS messages used in overpayment scams. They look like real UPI notifications but are designed to trick you into sending money back:

Fake Extra Payment Claim

This message claims an extra ₹500 was sent by mistake and asks for immediate refund. Notice how it mimics a real transaction SMS.

Urgent Refund Demand

Claims you received ₹1,000 instead of ₹500 and demands quick return of the difference. Scammers use urgency to prevent verification.

Mimicked Transaction Alert

Looks exactly like a genuine UPI SMS but includes a fake refund request. Always check your UPI app directly.

How to Prevent It

  • ✅ NEVER click on links in SMS messages claiming to be from your bank.
  • ✅ Verify all UPI transactions directly in your UPI app.
  • ✅ Check the sender's number - banks use official shortcodes (e.g., 56263).
  • ✅ Never share your UPI PIN or OTP with anyone.
  • ✅ Enable UPI app lock and use biometric authentication.
  • ✅ Report suspicious SMS to 1909 (DoT) or forward to 7726.
⚠️ REMEMBER: If someone claims to have sent you extra money by mistake, ALWAYS check your UPI app first. Never send refunds based on SMS alone.

In Case of Fraud?

If you have shared your UPI PIN or OTP accidentally:

  1. Immediately open your UPI app and change your UPI PIN.
  2. Call your bank's official helpline to block your UPI and account.
  3. Report the fraud to NPCI at 1800-111-4455 or email support@npci.org.in.
  4. File complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 (Helpline number).
  5. Take screenshots of the fake SMS and fraud details as evidence.
